## The principle

Nobody gets caught on the keyword. They get caught on the detail underneath it, usually within ninety seconds. Nobody reaches a client without a live conversation with me.

## Layer 1: bots and fakes

Software flags the obvious stuff first. Signals I see in real applications:

- Throwaway email domains. I block 22 of them.
- Auto-apply tools applying on someone's behalf.
- The same answer pasted into every screening question, sometimes with 26 job links in it.
- Two names, one phone number and near-identical CVs.
- A LinkedIn URL that resolves to no real profile.
- A phone number from one region with a claim of the residency the role requires.

Flags go to me for review. Software removes noise, it does not make the final call.

## Layer 2: the proof task

Every applicant who isn't filtered out gets a short task written for the role, stated openly in the job ad.

Example from a developer role in September 2026: applicants had to add a timestamp to the README of any of their GitHub repos and submit the link.

- 43 applications received.
- 40 skipped it or wrote "N/A".
- 3 did it and went straight to a video meeting with me.
- 3 went to a tech screen with the hiring manager.
- 2 were hired in 10 days, and 1 was still in play.

The instructions were right there in the job ad.

**Won't a proof task scare good people away?** It scares away people who weren't going to take the role seriously. The tasks are short and written for the role. People who actually want the job do them, and the shortlist gets better, not shorter.

## Layer 3: video answers

Short video answers shaped around the role. They show how a candidate explains a decision, describes their own contribution and connects their experience to the team. Communication the client can judge for themselves, and specific motivation instead of a generic cover letter.

## Layer 4: the video meeting with me

I test every CV claim: who owned the decision, what broke and what it cost. Inflated claims usually fall apart in about ninety seconds, and I write down what I found in my notes for the client.

Example, a senior project manager screen:

| CV claim | My check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| "8+ years in project delivery" | Dates against LinkedIn | Dates match |
| "End-to-end ownership of delivery" | Who made the call when it went wrong? | Probed |
| "Led delivery for enterprise clients" | What did you decide alone, and what did it cost? | Probed |
| "Stakeholder reporting, PMO tooling" | Detail underneath | Real and solid. It's a PMO job, not a PM one. |

## Layer 5: the evidence

- GitHub, portfolios or case studies where relevant.
- Individual contribution separated from team achievements.
- Unclear claims flagged for follow-up, not treated as facts.
- References and notice period confirmed.
